SIP · RFC 3261

Basic call

Alice calls Bob through a proxy. Signaling may hairpin at the proxy. Media often goes direct. The call is not up until ACK completes the 200 OK.

On a phone, swipe a wide ladder sideways. The steps below repeat the same sequence.

Sequence

  1. INVITE — Alice to Proxy. Early offer: SDP in INVITE
  2. INVITE — Proxy to Bob. Proxied toward Bob
  3. 100 Trying — Bob to Proxy. Hop-by-hop, not end-to-end
  4. 100 Trying — Proxy to Alice. The proxy generates its own
  5. 180 Ringing — Bob to Proxy. Early dialog if a To tag is set
  6. 180 Ringing — Proxy to Alice. Relayed toward Alice
  7. 200 OK — Bob to Proxy. SDP answer; dialog confirmed
  8. 200 OK — Proxy to Alice. Follows the Via path back
  9. ACK — Alice to Proxy. Separate transaction for a 2xx
  10. ACK — Proxy to Bob. Stays on path if Record-Routed
  11. RTP — Alice to Bob. Media may bypass the proxy
  12. BYE — Alice to Proxy. Alice hangs up
  13. BYE — Proxy to Bob. Same Call-ID and tags
  14. 200 OK — Bob to Proxy. BYE accepted
  15. 200 OK — Proxy to Alice. Dialog is gone

Dialogs

A dialog is identified by the Call-ID, the local tag, and the remote tag. The UAC puts a tag on From. The UAS puts a tag on To in a response. A provisional response with a To tag creates an early dialog. The dialog is confirmed when the UAC receives a 2xx to the INVITE.

This ladder is an early offer: Alice puts the SDP offer in the INVITE, and Bob puts the answer in the 200 OK. A delayed offer is the other shape: the INVITE has no SDP, the offer arrives in the 200 OK, and the answer comes back in the ACK.

What is hop-by-hop

  • 100 Trying is hop-by-hop. The proxy answers Alice so she stops retransmitting. Bob's 100, if he sends one, is for the proxy.
  • ACK for a 2xx is a new transaction and travels the dialog route set. If the proxy sent Record-Route, it stays on the ACK and the later BYE. If it did not, those requests go straight to the remote target in Contact.
  • ACK for a non-2xx final response, such as 486 Busy Here, belongs to the INVITE transaction. It is hop-by-hop and is not a separate end-to-end transaction.
  • BYE ends a confirmed dialog. The other side answers 200 OK. The Call-ID does not change.

Remember

180 Ringing means alerting, not answer. 200 OK means the far end accepted the session. ACK completes that handshake. RTP can start once the offer and answer are known, which on this ladder is when the 200 arrives.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the ACK and then watching the far end retransmit the 200 until the call drops.
  • Assuming media always follows the proxy because signaling did.
  • Reading a To tag on the 180 as proof the call was answered. That is an early dialog.

Sources

  • RFC 3261, sections 12 (dialogs), 13 (INVITE), 15 (BYE), and 17 (transactions)
  • Example: sip:alice@example.com at 192.0.2.10 calls sip:bob@example.com at 192.0.2.20
